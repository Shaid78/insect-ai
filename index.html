Can AI Predict How Climate Change Affects Insect Immunity?

We built a computer program that reads genetic data from insects and predicts how their immune systems change when the environment gets hotter or has more CO₂. Scroll down to see what we compared and how much better our method is than older approaches.

1 What is this page about?

A plain-language explanation before you see any numbers.

The problem: Insects fight infections using their immune system. But when the environment changes — like hotter temperatures or higher CO₂ — their immune system behaves differently. Scientists don't fully understand why.
Our solution: We built an artificial intelligence (AI) program that reads two kinds of data from insects:
  • Epigenetic data — chemical "switches" that turn genes on or off
  • Transcriptomic data — which genes are actually being used by the insect cell
The AI then compares this data across different environmental conditions and predicts which genes matter most.
Why it matters: If we understand how insect immunity responds to climate change, we can better predict disease outbreaks, protect pollinators like bees, and control pests that damage crops.

Analogy: Imagine a factory (the insect cell). The epigenetic data is like the instruction manual, and the transcriptomic data is like which machines are actually running. Our AI reads both and figures out which machines break first when it gets too hot.
